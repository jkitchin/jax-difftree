* Overview

~difftree~ is a JAX implementation of soft, oblique-split, gradient-trainable
decision trees with sklearn-compatible estimators. The package is built around
uncertainty quantification: regression leaves are heteroscedastic Gaussians (or
multivariate Gaussians for multi-output targets), and the predictive
distribution at each input is a mixture of Gaussians weighted by soft path
probabilities. This structure delivers a calibrated predictive variance, a
clean decomposition of uncertainty into within-leaf (aleatoric) and
between-leaf (routing) components, and, for multivariate outputs, closed-form
cross-conditioning and gradient-based inverse design as direct consequences.

The implementation is closest in spirit to the end-to-end deterministic-tree
formulation of cite:hehn2020end, with two practical departures motivated by
UQ. First, splits are kept finitely soft at test time rather than annealed to
hard, so soft-routing uncertainty contributes to the predictive variance.
Second, a small post-hoc calibration step, in the same family as temperature
scaling for classifier logits cite:guo2017calibration, jointly fits per-depth
steepness and a scalar variance correction on a held-out validation slice by
minimizing NLL.

The broader design space is reviewed in Ref. citenum:costa2023recent.

* Why differentiable trees

Traditional decision trees cite:breiman1984cart,quinlan1986induction grow
greedily and use hard, axis-aligned splits, which prevents joint optimization
of the whole tree and limits the splits to one feature at a time. Several
related lines of work make trees differentiable in some form:

- Deep Neural Decision Forests cite:kontschieder2015deep wrap a probabilistic routing tree around a deep feature extractor, but inference still evaluates every leaf for every sample, defeating the conditional-data-flow advantage of trees.
- Soft decision trees distilled from neural networks cite:frosst2017distilling and adaptive neural trees cite:tanno2019adaptive retain soft routing throughout, and do not target calibrated uncertainty directly.
- Ref. citenum:hehn2020end propose end-to-end training of trees by annealing the steepness $\gamma$ during training so that the final inference model is hard (single root-to-leaf path per sample) while training remains differentiable. This recovers the tree's $O(\log L)$ inference cost.

For UQ, the issue with annealing $\gamma \to \infty$ is that the soft-routing
component of predictive uncertainty is destroyed. ~difftree~ keeps $\gamma$
finite at test time and exposes the routing component explicitly, while still
supporting hard inference for users who want fast point predictions.

Non-greedy oblique training generally has been a productive direction
cite:norouzi2015efficient,murthy1994system, and the soft-routing formulation
fits naturally inside it.

* Quickstart

** Univariate regression

#+begin_example
from difftree import DifferentiableTreeRegressor
from sklearn.datasets import make_friedman1
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split

X, y = make_friedman1(n_samples=600, noise=1.0, random_state=0)
X_tr, X_te, y_tr, y_te = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=100, random_state=0)

model = DifferentiableTreeRegressor(depth=5, n_epochs=150, random_state=0)
model.fit(X_tr, y_tr)

mean = model.predict(X_te)
mean, std = model.predict(X_te, return_std=True)
mean, std, std_aleatoric, std_routing = model.predict(X_te, return_components=True)
samples = model.sample_y(X_te, n_samples=200)
q05, q50, q95 = model.predict_quantile(X_te, q=[0.05, 0.5, 0.95])
#+end_example

The entropy decomposition follows the standard mutual-information identity
$H[\mathbb{E}_\ell \pi_\ell] = \mathbb{E}_\ell H[\pi_\ell] + I[Y; \ell \mid x]$
used for separating aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty in Bayesian deep
learning cite:depeweg2018decomposition.

* Model

The tree has fixed depth $D$ with $2^D - 1$ internal nodes and $2^D$ leaves
in BFS order. Each internal node $i$ holds an oblique split parameterized by
$(w_i \in \mathbb{R}^p, b_i \in \mathbb{R})$. The split function is

\begin{equation*}
s_i(x) \;=\; \sigma\!\bigl(\gamma_d \, (w_i^{\top} x + b_i)\bigr),
\qquad
\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},
\end{equation*}

where $\gamma_d$ is a per-depth steepness. The probability that input $x$
reaches leaf $\ell$ is the product of $s_i$ or $1-s_i$ along the path,
computed in log space for numerical stability.

** Leaves

For univariate regression, leaf $\ell$ stores $(\mu_\ell, \sigma_\ell^2)$.
For multivariate regression, it stores $(\mu_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{d_y}, L_\ell)$
where $L_\ell$ is a Cholesky factor with positive-softplus diagonal so that
$\Sigma_\ell = L_\ell L_\ell^{\top}$ is positive-definite by construction. For
classification, leaves hold categorical logits.

** Predictive distribution

\begin{equation*}
p(y \mid x) \;=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{2^D} \mu_\ell(x) \, \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(y;\, \mu_\ell, \alpha\, L_\ell L_\ell^{\top}\bigr),
\end{equation*}

a conditional Gaussian mixture with input-dependent mixture weights, in the
spirit of mixture density networks cite:bishop1994mdn but with a
tree-structured parameterization of the mixture rather than a black-box neural
network. The scalar $\alpha = \exp(\log\alpha)$ is fitted post-hoc on a
held-out validation slice as an LLPR/temperature-scaling-style variance
correction.

** Variance decomposition

By the law of total variance,

\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Cov}[y \mid x]
\;=\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_\ell\bigl[\Sigma_\ell\bigr]}_{\text{aleatoric}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\mathrm{Cov}_\ell\bigl[\mu_\ell\bigr]}_{\text{routing}},
\end{equation*}

where the expectation and covariance are taken with respect to leaf index
$\ell$ weighted by the path probabilities $\mu_\ell(x)$. Both pieces are
exposed by ~predict(return_components=True)~ and by ~predict_full_cov~.

** Training

The loss is the negative marginal log-likelihood $-\log p(y \mid x)$ averaged
over the training set. Adam with global gradient-norm clipping is used. A
short warmup phase trains splits and leaf means with leaf variances frozen at
$\sigma_\ell^2 \approx 1$ to avoid the classic variance-collapse pathology of
heteroscedastic NLL training cite:lakshminarayanan2017simple. The steepness
$\gamma$ is annealed from $\gamma_{\text{init}}$ to $\gamma_{\max}$ on a fixed
per-epoch schedule during both phases, in the same spirit as cite:hehn2020end
except that $\gamma$ is not driven to infinity, preserving soft-routing UQ at
test time.

** Calibration

After training, splits and leaf parameters are frozen, and a small set of
calibration parameters (per-depth $\log\gamma$ and, for regressors,
$\log\alpha$) are fitted by Adam on the held-out validation slice by
minimizing NLL. This is the tree analogue of temperature scaling
cite:guo2017calibration, a low-dimensional post-hoc rescaling that targets
the predictive distribution's calibration without altering its mean structure.
Empirical coverage of the resulting prediction intervals tracks nominal
coverage closely on the test set, as demonstrated in the heteroscedastic-noise
notebook.

** Cross-conditioning and inverse design

For multivariate $y$, the conditional $p(y_{\text{unobs}} \mid x, y_{\text{obs}})$
is again a Gaussian mixture, with each leaf's component obtained by Schur
complement on $\Sigma_\ell$ and the leaf weights re-normalized by the marginal
density of $y_{\text{obs}}$ under each leaf. This recipe is standard for
Gaussian mixtures and gives closed-form conditional mean and covariance and
exact mixture sampling.

Inverse design exploits the fact that the tree's likelihood
$\log p(y_{\text{target}} \mid x)$ is end-to-end differentiable in $x$ since
splits and leaves are smooth. We optimize $x$ by Adam ascent with multiple
random restarts to handle the multimodality of the soft mixture surface.
Constrained variants where only a subset of outputs are targeted are
supported by passing a partial dict of target values.

* Limitations and caveats

- The fixed-depth full-binary structure makes JAX-friendly vectorization easy but caps practical depth around $D \le 10$ to $12$ because of the $2^D - 1$ split-parameter blowup. For larger problems, a grown-tree variant in the style of cite:hehn2020end can be added later as a separate fitter that reuses the same tree representation.
- Splits are oblique linear by default. Non-linear splits (small CNNs or feature-attention gates) fit the same training framework but are not yet exposed as a public API.
- Inverse design is local; multi-start ascent is the practical fallback for multimodality.
